# Xi Yue (西岳) — Master Reference

---

## Core Identity

Xi Yue is a poor, mountainous, militarized, export-survival civilization.

It is inspired by military states and hard frontier societies: Prussia, Sparta, Gurkha Nepal, Dagestani combat cultures, Cimmeria, and other places where hardship filters human beings.

Xi Yue is not merely a "warrior clan."

It is a society where violence is economic mobility.

The difference between being a mercenary and not being one may be the difference between a family eating meat or eating gruel.

**Divine Beast:** White Tiger  
**Peril Beast:** Qiongqi  
**Elements:** Metal and Wind  
**Weapon Class:** Daos / Sabers

---

## Ecology & Economy

Xi Yue is mountainous and not known for agriculture. The land is inhabitable but difficult. Its people are tough because life requires it.

The economy depends on:

- mercenaries
- military officers
- weapons
- armour
- war horses
- war dogs
- metals
- minerals
- military training
- assassins and spies
- diaspora networks

Xi Yue imports significantly from Nan Yan: grain, metals, and external refinement goods.

Historically, it relied heavily on Dong Chen as its largest employer. Xi Yue mercenaries acted as war dogs who helped deter Nan Yan from upsetting the balance. More recently, Xi Yue seeks to diversify reliance toward Nan Yan as well.

Economically, Xi Yue is underdeveloped. It produces dangerous people, not broad prosperity.

---

## Patron Sovereign — White Tiger

White Tiger is not a person or a caring deity. It is a cosmic force.

It embodies:

- metal
- west
- autumn
- severance
- discipline
- judgment
- contraction
- grief
- war
- cutting away
- reduction toward essence

White Tiger does not mollycoddle its people.

Its philosophy is brutally simple:

> Suffering and trial are the greatest gifts.

White Tiger does not rescue. White Tiger strips away shields and support. This produces the best survivors.

White Tiger should not be portrayed as sadistic. It is more terrifying if treated as impersonal cosmic law: metal does not hate ore, but refinement destroys weakness.

## Spiritual Framework

### False Clan Technique: 煞之道

**Sha Zhi Dao — The Way of Baleful Force**

Xi Yue never received its proper clan technique.

Qiongqi, enemy of White Tiger, disguised itself as White Tiger and gave the Xi Yue forefathers 煞之道.

This was an evil technique. It produced violence, killing intent, predatory instinct, and social brutality. It resulted in untold deaths and eventually disrupted the balance of the world.

But Qiongqi also inadvertently gave Xi Yue a terrible gift.

The people became:

- the best mercenaries
- the best spies
- the best assassins
- masters of survival networks
- experts in operating outside formal borders

### Corrective Path: 仁之路

**Ren Zhi Lu — The Road of Benevolence / Humaneness**

When 煞之道 became too destructive, Guan Yin intervened out of benevolence and taught 仁之路 as a counter-technique.

This did not erase Xi Yue's wound.

It prevented total civilizational self-destruction.

The pairing is therefore:

> 煞之道 — force without compassion  
> 仁之路 — compassion restraining force

Power is gained through killing, restraint, mercy, and understanding life and death. The dual path is shaped by life-and-death sword concepts: the killing sword and the life-giving sword.

### Dark Art: 断舍离

**Duàn Shě Lí — Cut, Release, Separate**

Spiritual minimalism. Emotional and mnemonic erasure. Letting go to the point of self-reduction.

## Society & Mobility

Xi Yue's baseline is subsistence.

If a family becomes rich, it is usually because someone survived:

- mercenary service
- military command
- elite guard work
- dangerous foreign contracts
- banditry
- assassination
- espionage

Even women are violent, not as a romantic warrior trope, but because the whole society is militarized and poverty touches everyone.

There are no elaborate flirtation rituals. Poverty filters all. Wealth and middle-income status show that a family survived the meat grinder.

Success in Xi Yue often means escaping Xi Yue.

A powerful mercenary or general tends to bring the family out of the country if possible.

---

## Diaspora

Xi Yue has no tightly enforced borders. Many failed soldiers, veterans, mercenaries, or ambitious people leave.

They often become:

- bandits in other lands
- mercenaries
- caravan guards
- spies
- assassins
- smugglers
- fixers
- private military operators
- foreign officers

The lowest bandit in Nan Yan may live better than a rich man in Xi Yue.

This creates a powerful diaspora.

Outwardly, Xi Yue is poor. But its network abroad is far stronger than outsiders understand. Its people are disproportionately present in dangerous, deniable, and strategically important roles.

Xi Yue's power is tactical, distributed, and human-network based — not total world control.

### Brain Drain

Xi Yue produces exceptional people but cannot retain them.

Anyone truly exceptional tends to leave.

This resembles Conan of Cimmeria: the homeland forges the person, but the person's greatness unfolds elsewhere.

Xi Yue's tragic contradiction:

> The mountains create blades, then lose them.

---

## The 10,000-Man Cannibal Prison

One of Xi Yue's most famous icons is the 10,000-man cannibal prison.

Annually, 10,000 men are sent into the prison. Only one emerges.

There is no food.

The prisoners are the food.

The event reduces prison numbers and creates an ultimate proving ground. Most participants are prisoners, but some are volunteers. There are very few volunteers, but they exist because social mobility in Xi Yue is so constrained that the gamble may appear rational.

The winner becomes a captain of the imperial guard.

This event is state-sanctioned filtration. It is the purest institutional expression of White Tiger logic:

> If one human can survive ten thousand, that survivor is strategically valuable.

---

## Protagonist Profile

A protagonist who volunteers for the cannibal prison is not an innocent chosen one.

He chose the meat grinder because the alternatives were worse.

This implies:

- desperation
- clarity
- family pressure
- ambition
- poverty
- willingness to face the true logic of civilization

He should not necessarily survive through brute strength alone. The prison rewards total survivability:

- intelligence
- restraint
- adaptability
- temporary alliances
- betrayal recognition
- starvation psychology
- emotional control
- willingness to learn what humans become under pressure

He emerges marked forever.

## Relationships to Other Clans

### Dong Chen

Dong Chen historically employed Xi Yue heavily. Xi Yue was both useful and dependent. Xi Yue views this with pride mixed with the awareness of subordination.

### Nan Yan

Nan Yan is richer, more dynamic, and full of opportunity. It attracts Xi Yue migrants, mercenaries, bandits, and ambitious survivors. Xi Yue covets what it lacks and supplies what Nan Yan will not do itself.

### Bei Ming

Bei Ming represents something stranger than Xi Yue hardship. Xi Yue survives pressure; Bei Ming seeks to transcend ordinary existence.

## Apotheosis

Xi Yue's terminal singularity is:

**Singularity of Un-Creation / Wuji**

Primordial emptiness, unmaking reality, and severance from cause and effect.

White Tiger cuts so deeply that:

- form dissolves
- causality dissolves
- differentiation dissolves
- reality is severed back toward pre-creation

This is not mere destruction. It is un-creation.

## Core Tragedy

Xi Yue's virtue is survivability.

Its pathology is that survivability becomes institutionalized brutality.

Its deepest contradiction:

> It produces the hardest people in the world, but cannot give them lives worth staying for.
